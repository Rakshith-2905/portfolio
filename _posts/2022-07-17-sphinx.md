*ICML 2022, Spotlight · [Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/subramanyam22a.html) · [Code](https://github.com/Rakshith-2905/SPHInX) · [Talk](https://icml.cc/virtual/2022/spotlight/17162)*

**Rakshith Subramanyam, Vivek Narayanaswamy, Mark Naufel, Andreas Spanias, Jayaraman J. Thiagarajan**

A pre-trained StyleGAN-v2 is a powerful image prior. If you can find the latent code that reproduces an image (*inversion*), you can denoise it, recover it from a few measurements, or edit it semantically. That works well for images like the generator's training data, such as FFHQ faces. For **out-of-distribution** images, faces in the wild, retina scans, skin lesions or X-rays, existing inversion methods produce poor reconstructions or brittle latents that fall apart under small perturbations.

## The idea

**SPHInX** (StyleGAN with Projection Heads for Inverting X) learns small networks around a *frozen* StyleGAN instead of optimizing a single latent code:

1. **Style projection head.** Replaces StyleGAN's mapping network. A shared bottleneck (512 to 256 to 16) feeds a separate small decoder for each of the 18 style layers, which decouples the layers of the W+ latent space.
2. **Vicinal regularization.** Every iteration samples a fresh noise input, so the head learns a whole neighborhood of latents that map to the image, not one fragile point. That is what makes the result robust to perturbation and usable for editing.
3. **Optional content head and noise.** A small convolutional head can replace StyleGAN's constant input, and per-layer noise can be optimized, for images far from faces.

Training uses MSE plus LPIPS for 10,000 iterations per image with the FFHQ 1024×1024 generator kept frozen.

## What it does

- **Faces in the wild:** under rotation, scaling and translation, SPHInX gives higher PSNR than Image2StyleGAN, pSp, BDInvert and other inversion methods in every setting.
- **Non-face images with a face GAN:** on AFHQ animals, diabetic retinopathy scans, ISIC skin lesions and MIMIC chest X-rays it beats the baselines on both PSNR and LPIPS.
- **Restoration:** it denoises non-face images using only the face prior, and recovers medical images from 1–5% of random measurements at around 34 dB PSNR.
- **Editing:** it supports semantic edits and interpolation on images from new domains, such as cartoons.

## Why it matters

Strong generative priors are only useful if they hold up on data they weren't trained on. SPHInX was my first paper on that theme, making a model behave sensibly out of distribution, which the rest of my PhD kept coming back to.
