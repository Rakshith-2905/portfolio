*WACV 2023 · [Paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2023/html/Subramanyam_Contrastive_Knowledge-Augmented_Meta-Learning_for_Few-Shot_Classification_WACV_2023_paper.html) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12346) · [Code](https://github.com/Rakshith-2905/CAML)*

**Rakshith Subramanyam, Mark Heimann, T.S. Jayram, Rushil Anirudh, Jayaraman J. Thiagarajan**

Few-shot learning asks a model to recognize new classes from one to five examples. Meta-learners like MAML learn an initialization that adapts quickly, but real task streams are heterogeneous: birds one day, aircraft or textures the next. A single initialization struggles, and newer methods that add task-specific structure still adapt each task largely in isolation.

**CAML** gives the meta-learner a memory. It maintains a knowledge graph that summarizes the tasks it has seen, and uses it to decide how to modulate the base learner for each new task.

## How it works

1. **Encode the task.** Class prototypes (averaged ResNet-18 embeddings of the support examples) form a small graph for the current task.
2. **Consult the knowledge graph.** The task graph is connected to a meta knowledge graph of past experience, and a **contrastive distillation** loss pulls relevant knowledge into the task encoding.
3. **Modulate the learner.** The knowledge-enhanced encoding gates the shared initialization, giving each task its own starting point before MAML's inner-loop adaptation.
4. **Update the memory.** The knowledge graph is updated with a moving average after each task, so it accumulates history without being overwritten.

## Results

Evaluated on Meta-Dataset (birds, textures, aircraft, fungi, flowers, traffic signs) and DomainNet, against MAML, MuMo-MAML and ARML (mean accuracy, 5-way):

| Setting | ARML | CAML |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard few-shot, 1-shot (avg of 4 datasets) | 48.70 | **49.43** |
| Standard few-shot, 5-shot | 62.00 | **62.68** |
| Multi-domain (DomainNet), 1-shot | 46.01 | **48.01** |
| Multi-domain (DomainNet), 5-shot | 63.19 | **65.80** |
| Unseen dataset (leave-one-out), 1-shot | 36.65 | **38.93** |
| Unseen dataset (leave-one-out), 5-shot | 49.50 | **53.10** |

The gains grow as tasks get more different from training. On standard few-shot they're marginal; when the test task comes from a dataset never seen in training they reach up to 5 points (1-shot) and 10 points (5-shot), for example on traffic signs.

## Why it matters

Using structured outside knowledge to adapt better with little data is the same move I later made with language: in CREPE with CLIP prompts, and in DECIDER with LLM-generated attributes.
